title: Exterior noise
description: The roads and railways around a project and the traffic data an exterior noise assessment needs.
---

Open **Sound insulation** in the project bar. It lists the project's noise sources: the roads and railways whose noise reaches the facades, each with the inputs an emission calculation needs. Richtfest collects and requests these inputs and calculates draft road rating levels (RLS-19) and relevant exterior noise levels per facade (DIN 4109-2). It does not calculate rail levels (Schall 03); those come from a study.

## Mapped roads and railways

**Look up roads and railways** runs the [environmental survey](/docs/workflows/project-onboarding#environmental-survey) and lists what the state's official sources map within about 1 km, nearest first (ATKIS Basis-DLM in most states; in Berlin the Detailnetz street network and the source layers of the strategic noise map 2022):

- **Roads**: name, class (Bundesautobahn, Bundesstraße, Landes-, Kreis- or Gemeindestraße, non-public), number such as B 43, lanes and carriageway width. ATKIS splits a street into several objects; named streets of one class are shown once. At most 150 roads are listed: classified or counted roads keep up to half of the places even when they are further out than the nearest local streets.
- **Railways**: VzG route number (what DB InfraGO answers train volume requests by), category (passenger, freight, S-Bahn, tram, U-Bahn), lines, single or double track and electrification.

The distance runs from the address point to the nearest mapped axis, not from a facade.

### Coverage by state

| State | Roads and railways | Site point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Berlin | Detailnetz + strategic noise map 2022 | address register (house) |
| Rheinland-Pfalz | ATKIS (OGC API) | imported location (house) |
| Baden-Württemberg, Thüringen | ATKIS, own flat schema | ALKIS address point (house) / parcel (TH) |
| Hessen, Nordrhein-Westfalen, Niedersachsen, Bremen, Bayern, Sachsen-Anhalt, Brandenburg, Mecklenburg-Vorpommern, Schleswig-Holstein | ATKIS (WFS) | house coordinates (HE, NW, BB), building outline (NI, HB), parcels (ST), OpenStreetMap Nominatim (BY, MV, SH) |
| Hamburg, Sachsen, Saarland | not connected (no open source with road classes; Hamburg's ATKIS service is broken) | house (HH, SL) / parcels (SN) |

The state is found from the municipality name (BKG VG250 municipality list) and confirmed by the site point lying inside that state; an ambiguous name is checked in every candidate state and fails rather than guessing. Street and house number must match exactly ("Str." and "Straße" are treated alike); a nearby number is never used. In Bayern, Mecklenburg-Vorpommern and Schleswig-Holstein, which have no open exact address service, the point comes from OpenStreetMap's Nominatim, marked as approximate (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL); `NOMINATIM_URL` sets the instance and an empty value turns it off. The saved survey records the point's precision and source. Where no road and rail source is connected, the lookup says so; add sources by hand.

In **Berlin** (Geoportal Berlin, dl-de/zero-2-0), roads come from Detailnetz with the strategic noise map's published traffic count of the segment nearest the site (Kfz/24h and Lkw/24h, usually Verkehrsmengenkarte 2019). Railways are the noise map's mapped tracks: **S- und Fernbahn** as one source without route or line numbers, **Straßenbahn** and **U-Bahn (oberirdisch)**; train volumes and speeds are not published there. Adding a Berlin road with a count fills **daily traffic**, **heavy vehicle share** and **traffic basis** as imported values with the source as their basis; check and overwrite them like any other value.

### Traffic counts

After the state's roads and railways, the lookup attaches published counts. Berlin's own counts come first; then imported count stations and sections (BASt permanent and census stations on Autobahnen and Bundesstraßen, and the NRW, Niedersachsen and Sachsen road census, see [traffic counts](/docs/architecture/public-data#traffic-counts)); then the nationwide noise mapping below. A count station attaches to roads within 30 m with the same number (both carriageways), a section to a road it runs along within 15 m. Among several, the most recent year wins, then state section over BASt permanent station over BASt census point, then the one nearest the site. Imported counts include heavy vehicles.

- **Roads**: the Umweltbundesamt's strategic noise mapping (Lärmkartierung, roads above 3 million vehicles a year), matched to the road it runs along (at least 60 % within 15 m and within 30° of parallel). Vehicles per year ÷ 365 give Kfz/24h; there is no heavy-vehicle split. Berlin's own counts take precedence. A value of exactly 3,000,300 vehicles a year, which appears as a threshold placeholder, is ignored.
- **Railways**: the Eisenbahn-Bundesamt's noise mapping (round 4, 2022) for main lines, then the Umweltbundesamt's rail sources, matched by DB route number first and otherwise by a line running alongside. Trains per year are split into day (06–18), evening (18–22) and night (22–06); the source's vehicle categories VK1–VK4 are kept as labels, not translated into Schall 03 train types. Trams and U-Bahn get no count.

Adding a counted road fills **daily traffic** (and **heavy vehicle share** where known) and **traffic basis**; adding a counted railway fills **train volumes** and **traffic basis**. These are imported values with the source as their basis; check and overwrite them like any other value. Speeds, surfaces and train types still come from the authority or operator. Hessen and several other states publish no open counts for local streets, so these are entered or requested.

Choose **Add** to make a mapped road or railway a noise source; Richtfest keeps a snapshot of what was mapped. **Add road** and **Add railway** add a source by hand.

## Suggested sources near the building

Above the mapped list, **Suggested sources near the building** proposes the mapped roads and railways that lie within a class-based screening radius and are not added yet:

| Class | Radius |
| --- | --- |
| Motorway (Bundesautobahn) | 1000 m |
| Federal road | 500 m |
| State and district road, municipal road with 4+ lanes | 300 m |
| Municipal and other public road | 100 m |
| Railway (main line, S-Bahn, freight) | 1000 m |
| Tram and U-Bahn only | 300 m |

Distances run from the placed plan when one is stored in [3D location](/docs/workflows/project-onboarding#3d-location-editor), else from the official building footprint at the address, else from the address point. A source also qualifies when the address point lies within its radius, so an approximate placement does not drop it; the row then names both distances. Private roads and roads without a class are not suggested.

These radii are Richtfest's screening heuristic for completeness, not a norm rule. Review each suggestion and choose **Add**, or **Add all**. The same suggestions appear under **Suggestions** in 3D location, highlighted in amber on the map.

## Facade levels

**Facade levels** shows, once the plan is placed in 3D location and its exterior walls are known, a draft of the levels a Schallschutznachweis starts from. It is marked **Draft, not reviewed**; the planner checks it before it is used.

**Sources** lists every source with its status:

- **Calculated**: a road with traffic volume, and a speed limit except on motorways. The row shows hourly traffic and truck shares day and night, the speed used and the emission level L'W, followed by every assumption: standard hourly traffic and truck shares for the road class when only DTV is known, the reference surface when no surface type is set, no gradient, two lanes, 3.5 m lanes and 50 km/h on municipal roads without a limit.
- **From study**: a railway with rating levels day and night entered from a study.
- **Inputs missing**, **No mapped axis**: not part of the levels until completed. Road levels need the mapped axis from the survey.

**Storeys.** The facades of each storey come from that storey's own plan in the building model. Only one plan is placed on the site; the plans of the other storeys are aligned to it by their building outline: Richtfest matches the corners of their exterior walls to the placed plan, so a plan drawn elsewhere on its sheet or a set-back upper storey still lands in place. The aligned plans are named above the tables. A storey plan that does not fit the placed outline (another building, a section) is left out and named under the limitations; a storey without a plan of its own repeats the facades of the placed plan. The receiver height follows each storey's **level** from the building model.

**Facades** lists, per storey, each exterior wall with the rating level of each source, La day, La night and La for sleeping rooms, which is what the room requirement uses. The rules applied are those of DIN 4109-2 for calculated levels: the rail reduction, the night surcharge for sleeping rooms, sources combined per period with the addition applied once to the sum, and sleeping rooms taking the higher of day and night. A facade turned away from all sources takes the loudest facade of its storey less the reduction for turned-away facades in open development (**Turned away**).

Limitations shown under the table: free propagation without screening by other buildings, flat ground, no reflections, no junction corrections, receivers at the ceiling of each storey with an assumed storey height of 3 m. Each makes the level more conservative or needs a check where it does not. The coefficients are configured on the server ([acoustic norm pack](/docs/engineering/deployment#acoustic-norm-pack)); the editions used are named above the tables.

## Exterior noise verification

**Exterior noise verification** turns the facade levels into the inputs of the DIN 4109-1 exterior noise requirement, room by room, and calculates every room whose inputs are complete. It works on the rooms of every storey plan in the building model: the placed plan and each plan aligned to it (see **Storeys** above). With more than one storey, rooms carry the storey in their name, for example *EG · Wohnen*, and the Nachweis covers all storeys in one calculation.

1. Above the rooms, choose the **Method** (standard exterior noise method or special assessment, for example near airports), whether **Flanking transmission** is negligible, and each storey's **Clear room height**.
2. Give each room its **Use**: sleeping room, living room, office or practice, hospital bedroom, or not protected (kitchen, bathroom, hall, storage). A proposal from the room name can be taken over with one click. Sleeping rooms use La for sleeping rooms, all others La by day.
3. **Edit facades** lists the room's exterior walls: the length along each wall comes from the room and wall outlines of the plan, times the clear room height. Enter the wall's **Rw** with its basis, and add each **window or door** with its area and installed Rw (corrections included) and basis. Windows and doors that **Derive from plan** found in the building model are listed already with their plan width; give them a height or area and their Rw. Ventilators, roller shutter boxes and similar parts are **small elements**: enter their count and Dn,e,w instead of an area. The wall area is the facade less its openings.
   Under **Roof surfaces**, add each roof that bounds the room (top storey, sloped ceiling) with its construction, area, Rw and basis. A roof takes the loudest La of the room's facades for the room's period; a room with only a roof is calculated too.
4. Confirm that **all exterior walls, roofs and openings of this room are entered**.

The result shows the required value (base requirement plus area correction K_AL), the provided value (R'w,ges less the 2 dB prognosis allowance) and the reserve, or what is still missing.

### Let the Richtfest Agent fill the gaps

Ask the Richtfest Agent in the project conversation, for example *"Prepare the exterior noise verification"* or *"Take the windows from the Fensterliste"*. It sees the same verification: which rooms are calculated and which inputs are missing. It reads the project sources (plans, Fensterliste, Baubeschreibung, test certificates, sections) and suggests what they state:

- the use of each room, from the plan's room names;
- windows and doors per room and exterior wall, with their area from the Fensterliste or plan dimensions, and their Rw where a source states it;
- small elements such as ventilators and roller shutter boxes, with their count and the Dn,e,w their data sheet states;
- the Rw of exterior walls and its basis, the construction, the clear room height, and the method and flanking choice.

Every suggestion cites the source page and a short quote. Suggestions never replace a value someone entered or confirmed, and a rejected suggestion is not proposed again. The agent does not estimate acoustic values: where no source states a value, it asks for exactly that input and points to this view.

**Suggestions to confirm** lists the suggested values above the rooms with their source. **Accept** or **Reject** each one, or **Accept all**. Suggested values are calculated right away and marked *Suggested*; until they are confirmed, the Nachweis stays a *Teilstand* and lists them as open items. The agent can also create the draft Nachweis when asked; the planner checks and signs it.

### Wall and window Rw from the component catalogue

**Suggest Rw from the component catalogue** (next to the room count) rates every exterior wall of the verification that has no Rw yet as a single-leaf massive wall: area mass from the wall's thickness and density, then the mass law of its material (concrete, calcium silicate, clay, lightweight or aerated concrete). Masonry takes the lowest wall density any mortar allows for its density class, and plaster is left out, so the value errs low. The value and its basis (method, area mass, assumptions) become a suggestion citing the pages its material, thickness and density came from.

Walls the method does not cover get no value and say why: insulated or multi-leaf build-ups (ETICS, facing leaves, filled units), perforated units outside the catalogue's limits, timber or drywall, or a missing thickness or density. Enter those from the manufacturer's data or test certificate. The agent uses the same function when asked.

The same button rates every window without Rw as a single window with insulating glass after DIN 4109-35: the highest catalogue row its glazing build-up (**Glazing**, written as in a window schedule: `6/16/4`, `4/12/4/12/4`, laminated `44.2`) or its declared glass Rw and its rebate seals meet, plus the corrections for a wood-aluminium frame, a small frame share, twin sash, fixed glazing, small windows, large panes and glazing bars. Enter the glazing when adding a window; frame, seals, opening, frame share and glazing bars are window fields in the building model. A condition nobody entered is taken at its least favourable: unknown seals count as one, an unknown opening takes the lower of the twin-sash and fixed-glazing corrections, and a window larger than the pane limit is assumed to hold a pane that large. The basis names the row, each correction and what was assumed. Windows with a declared or tested Rw (test certificate, DIN EN 14351-1 declaration) do not need the catalogue: enter that Rw instead. Windows below the catalogue's first row, single glazing, coupled and box windows, and windows with integrated ventilators are not rated.

### In the building model

The same values are editable in the [building model](/docs/workflows/project-onboarding): select a room for its **Exterior noise use** and **Exterior surfaces complete**, an exterior wall or roof for **Rw** and **Rw basis**, a window or door for **Opening area**, **Rw** and its basis, a window also for **Glazing**, **Glass Rw**, **Frame**, **Rebate seals**, **Opening**, **Frame share** and **Glazing bars**, a small element for **Element count** and **Dn,e,w**, a roof for **Surface area**. A wall lists the openings set into it, a window or door its wall. **Sound insulation → Exterior noise** holds the method, flanking and each storey's **clear room height**.

**Create Nachweis** writes the draft Schallschutznachweis Außenlärm as a document with PDF, without AI: a review summary, room overview, one calculation sheet per room with numbered input evidence (floor areas and facade lengths cite the plan page, entered values are marked as entries), the open inputs, an annex deriving La (sources, traffic, assumptions, facade levels, limits) and a sign-off block. While rooms are open it is titled *Teilstand*. Review and approve it like any other document; recreate it after inputs change. Values entered here are ordinary building-model values (entered, unreviewed until a person reviews them in the building model). The table is a draft: the plan model's rooms and walls may be incomplete; check facade lengths against the plan before confirming a room.

## Measure facade distances on the site

In **3D location** the scene layers include:

- **Roads & railways**: the mapped axes from the survey; sources already added are highlighted. Surveys run before axes were kept need **Look up again**.
- **Rail noise (EBA, Lden)**: the federal rail noise map (Eisenbahn-Bundesamt, Lärmkartierung Schiene, dl-de/by-2-0) for 2 × 2 km around the site, with its level bands. Only mapped main lines appear; no contour does not mean quiet. **Surroundings** shows the whole window from above.
- **Plan on site**: the first plan page with walls and a printed scale (ground floor first), drawn at its printed scale. Exterior walls are darker.

To place the plan, choose **Align plan** and click four points: a plan corner, the same corner on the site, a second plan corner far from the first, and that corner on the site. Clicks snap to wall corners and to official building corners. The plan is turned and moved, never rescaled; the **scale check** (site length ÷ plan length) shows how well printed scale and picks agree, with a note above 2 %. **Save placement** stores it as approximate; **Mark reviewed** records that someone checked it.

**Facade distances** then lists, per added road or railway, the shortest horizontal distance from an exterior wall to its mapped axis and which facade it faces (all walls are measured while none is marked exterior). **Use as distance** writes it to the source's **Distance to facade** with a basis naming plan, page, facade and placement state; the same value is offered under that input in Exterior noise. Mapped axes are not lane or track centrelines, so check the value against the planning requirement before relying on it.

### Take over a manual measurement

Without a placed plan, or for a source without mapped axes, measure directly: press **M** in 3D location and click the facade and the road or track (on the aerial image). In the floating panel, optionally describe the measurement, choose **Facade distance to road** or **Facade distance to railway** as its purpose, then **Save measurement**. Only then is **Use for exterior noise…** offered. General, neighboring-building and access-width measurements have no noise action; descriptions are not classified by keywords.

Expand that follow-up and explicitly choose **Use as distance to facade** to write the horizontal distance to a matching noise source, or to create a new source of the chosen kind. The list also offers matching mapped roads or railways within 40 m of the measured points that are not sources yet; choosing one adds it on adoption, not on measurement save. The nearest matching source or mapped axis is preselected. The basis records the saved measurement's ID and description, both points with their coordinates and that the value is a model observation, not surveyed. Changing or removing the saved measurement does not silently change an already adopted noise input; adopt a new value explicitly when needed.

## Request traffic data

**Request traffic data** creates an [inquiry](/docs/workflows/inquiries) for the chosen sources with the inputs still missing that an authority or operator supplies (not the facade distance). Ask the road traffic authority or Hessen Mobil for roads and DB InfraGO for railways; send the text yourself. The inquiry counts an item as answered once the value is entered on the source.
